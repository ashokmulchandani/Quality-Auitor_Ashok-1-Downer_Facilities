The 10-step auditor's loop

The full protocol, spoken as a ~2-minute interview answer: "My loop starts with the schedule, not the site — I plan pre-inspections to meet the Contract KPI minimums, then every visit follows the same spine: inspect to the standard, raise a BIF for every non-conformance, close it with an owner and a date, and feed the trend into the monthly report."

  1. Plan to KPI. Schedule planned pre-inspections across the zone to meet the Contract KPI minimums — then post-inspections on every pre-inspection, and WIP/post inspections to the zone's targets.
  2. Inspect to the standard, not to opinion. Workmanship, site safety, contractor compliance against the contract, the SWMS and the Work Order. Criteria are written down before the visit (ITPs, checklists).
  3. Raise a BIF for every non-conformance. Specifics: what, where, which requirement it breaches, evidence attached, one owner, one due date.
  4. Verify the claim. Subcontractor claims are verified against the inspection record — only conforming work gets paid.
  5. Root cause with the crew. 5 Whys, Fishbone, ICAM — with the people who did the work, not at them.
  6. Close corrective AND preventive actions. Track to completion; escalate in writing before the due date if anything slips.
  7. Record everything in WMS. Inspections, investigations, communications — saved against the specific Work Order, updated as actions complete.
  8. Investigate incidents to the same spine. Zero Harm/OHS, Code of Conduct, Environmental — make safe, investigate, distribute, close the Homes NSW notices (CIR/CIN/NCN).
  9. Report monthly with trend analysis. The monthly Inspection & Auditing Report: all inspections plus commentary and non-conformance trends — formally presented.
  10. Feed the audit focus. Trends from this month shape the next month's Inspection & Audit Focus — one BIF becomes a prevention.
🔑
The golden rule: if it's not in WMS, it didn't happen. The two orderings interviewers probe: make safe BEFORE documenting (incidents), and escalate in writing BEFORE the due date (actions). Say both the right way round.

🛠️ Site inspections — the contract's compliance engine

The PD's first KRA: pre-inspections, post-inspections, WIP inspections and subcontractor claim verification — all to Contract KPI targets for the zone, all feeding the monthly report.

Schedule to the KPI, not to the week

The inspection program is planned: pre-inspections meeting the Contract KPI requirements as a minimum, post-inspections on all of them, plus WIP and post inspections to hit the zone's targets — and additional inspections per the monthly Inspection & Audit Focus as directed by the Assurance and SDP Manager.

  • The KPI is the floor, not the ceiling — the monthly focus adds direction
  • WIP inspections catch defects while they're still cheap to fix
  • The schedule lives in WMS so every visit links to a Work Order
Interview nuance: "meeting Contract KPI requirements as a minimum" is the PD's exact wording — quote it back. They're testing whether you've read the document.

The pre-inspection — what you're actually checking

A pre-inspection is planned, announced and criteria-driven. On a housing maintenance job you check four things:

  • Workmanship — against the trade standard and the scope: is the repair done right? (AS/NZS 3000 electrical, AS/NZS 5601 gas, AS/NZS 3500 plumbing, NCC…)
  • Site safety — SWMS on site and followed, housekeeping, PPE, safe work method actually in use
  • Contractor compliance — licensed trades, inductions done, contract obligations met
  • Scope & progress — the work matches the Work Order; progress against the schedule
Rule of thumb: inspect to the written criteria — the PD asks the auditor to assess "workmanship, site safety and contractor compliance". Trade-standard fluency is what separates an auditor from a box-ticker.

BIFs — the contract's corrective-action engine

Every non-conformance found during inspections becomes a Business Improvement Form. A good BIF is complete and specific:

  • What was found, where, against which requirement (contract clause, standard, SWMS, work order)
  • Evidence — photos, measurements, records
  • Root cause (5 Whys / Fishbone run with the subcontractor)
  • Corrective AND preventive actions, one owner, one due date
  • Followed up to completion — outstanding corrective actions are the contract's biggest pain point
Why "BIF" matters: Downer's name for the NCR is a Business Improvement Form — the framing is improvement, not blame. Mirror that framing in the interview.

Post-inspections — the loop that makes pre-inspections mean something

The PD requires a post-inspection on all pre-inspections: verify the corrective actions actually landed, the BIF is closed with evidence, and the work now conforms. WIP and post inspections round out the month's coverage to the KPI targets.

  • A pre-inspection without a post-inspection is half a loop
  • Closure means verified on site — not "the subcontractor said it's done"
  • Update WMS against the Work Order as actions are confirmed complete

Subcontractor claim verification

The claim is checked against the inspection record: work complete, conforming to the ITP and scope, defects closed, evidence attached. No verification without site evidence.

  • Only conforming work gets paid — claim verification protects the contract's money
  • Discrepancies go to the BIF/defect process, then back to the claim
  • Your edge: you standardised ITPs across 50+ vendors for construction clients, cutting discrepancies 40%
Commercial angle: the PD says the auditor represents Downer Homes to resolve contractual issues with Homes NSW — claim verification is where quality discipline meets commercial discipline.

The monthly Inspection & Auditing Report

The month closes with a formal report: all inspections completed in the zone, with commentary and trend analysis of non-conformances raised — then formally presented monthly or as directed.

  • Commentary = what the numbers mean (not just the numbers)
  • Trend analysis = which non-conformances recur, by trade, by subcontractor, by type
  • The trends feed the next month's Inspection & Audit Focus — the loop closes
  • Your evidence: automated dashboards cut your reporting lead time 70% in previous roles

🧾 The BIF lifecycle — step by step

"Raise Business Improvement Forms for all non-conformances identified during inspections and follow-up completion of outstanding corrective actions." How that actually runs. Click each step.

STEP 1 Raise with specifics
The BIF records what, where, against which requirement — contract clause, trade standard, SWMS, Work Order — with evidence attached (photos, measurements). A vague BIF dies in argument; a specific one closes.
STEP 2 Assign owner + due date
One owner — the subcontractor supervisor, DSS or process owner — and one due date. No committee ownership: "everyone's job" means nobody's job.
STEP 3 Root cause with the crew
5 Whys, Fishbone or ICAM with the people who did the work. The corrective action fixes the defect; the preventive action fixes the cause.
STEP 4 Follow up to completion
Track outstanding corrective actions — this is a named PD duty. Escalate in writing before the due date to the SDP Manager and Regional Manager if anything threatens it.
STEP 5 Verify and close
Closure is verified on site (the post-inspection), recorded in WMS against the Work Order, and the BIF is closed with evidence.
STEP 6 Trend it
The BIF's type, trade and subcontractor feed the monthly trend analysis — and next month's audit focus. One BIF is a case; three are a pattern.
🏠
Downer angle: BIFs on a housing contract carry a tenant — a non-conformance means a family living with the defect. Closure speed is tenant welfare, not just compliance. That's the framing that lands in the interview.

🚨 Incident investigation & reporting

The PD's second KRA: investigate and report all Zero Harm/OHS, Code of Conduct and Environmental incidents in the zone — including corrective and preventive actions, followed up to completion, and Homes NSW's notices (CIR/CIN/NCN) closed and signed off.

First hour: make it safe

Stop or isolate the work, get people clear, notify the supervisor on site. Making safe comes before documenting — the ordering trap interviewers probe.

  • Preserve the scene for the investigation (WHS Act s 39 for notifiable incidents — no disturbance before the inspector arrives)
  • Notifiable incidents — death, serious injury/illness, dangerous incident — must be notified to SafeWork NSW immediately (WHS Act ss 35–38)
  • Support any injured person and bystanders first
Say it the right way round: make safe → notify → preserve → investigate. Never investigate → document → make safe.

Report and notify

Report through the system immediately: incident recorded in WMS against the Work Order, Zero Harm report raised, and internal notifications per the contract's escalation rules.

  • Escalate in writing before the due date anything that may delay the investigation and reporting (PD duty)
  • Notifiable incidents go to SafeWork NSW immediately — internal notification never replaces it
  • Your evidence: a real-time incident dashboard cut investigation time from 72 hours to under 24 in a previous role

Investigate to root cause

Lead with 5 Whys, Fishbone, fault-tree analysis or ICAM — with the people involved in the room. The investigation covers Zero Harm/OHS, Code of Conduct and Environmental incidents alike.

  • ICAM (Incident Cause Analysis Method) is the standard AU industry approach — organisational factors, not just the person
  • A good investigation finds the system failure, not the scapegoat
  • Complete reports by the requested completion dates — Homes NSW's dates or the internal ones, whichever binds
The PD's full scope: investigations cover OHS, Code of Conduct AND Environmental incidents — a Code of Conduct complaint is investigated with the same rigour as an injury.

Corrective AND preventive actions

The report names corrective actions (fix what happened) and preventive actions (stop it recurring) — each with an owner and a due date, followed up to completion.

  • The PD is explicit: "documenting the required corrective and preventive actions and following up of those actions to completion"
  • Preventive actions are where Zero Harm actually happens — the fix for the system, not the event
  • Update WMS as actions are confirmed complete

Distribute and close the loop

The PD names the distribution list — completed reports go to:

  • Contract Manager · Assurance & SDP Manager · Regional Managers
  • Homes NSW Managers (where directed) · Helpdesk Manager (where relevant)
  • Tenants are advised of investigation outcomes where they're involved
  • Homes NSW's CIR, CIN & NCN notices are completed and signed off
Why the distribution list matters: quoting it in the interview proves you've read the PD — it's the kind of detail that separates a rehearsed answer from a researched one.

WMS hygiene and trends

All investigation and inspection reports, details and communications are saved into WMS against the specific Work Order, updated as actions complete. Then the auditor's value-add:

  • Identify incident-related non-conformance trends and assist developing the monthly audit focuses
  • One NCN is a case; three are a pattern — the pattern is what the audit focus chases
  • Tenant outcomes communicated — closure isn't closed until the people are told
Where compliance dies: incidents closed in the system but not learned from. The trend analysis in the monthly report is what turns incidents into prevention.

🦺 Zero Harm & the NSW WHS framework

Zero Harm is Downer's safety brand — integrated into The Downer Standard under ISO 45001, with Cardinal Rules for critical risks. The PD's Safety KRA mirrors the NSW WHS regime: the WHS Act 2011 (NSW), WHS Regulation 2017 (NSW), and SafeWork NSW (the PD's "WorkCover" — WorkCover NSW became SafeWork NSW in 2015).

Risk levels & the hierarchy of controls

Low · Monitor

Routine maintenance risks

  • General housekeeping, minor repairs, admin controls sufficient
  • JSA/SWMS may be simple; standard procedures apply
  • Auditor verifies the control exists and is used
Medium · Control

Trade work with exposure

  • Electrical, gas, working at height on single-storey, hot work
  • SWMS required for high-risk construction work (Reg 291 list)
  • Controls must be job-specific, not a generic template
High · Stop & verify

High-risk construction work

  • Falls >2 m, asbestos, confined spaces, demolition, live services — the Reg 291 categories
  • SWMS mandatory, licence checks, supervision matched to capability
  • Auditor's call: make safe first, then investigate
🪜
The hierarchy of controls (WHS Reg 36) — say it in order: 1. Eliminate the hazard → 2. Substitute with something safer → 3. Isolate people from it → 4. Engineering controls → 5. Administrative controls (procedures, training, SWMS) → 6. PPE (last line, never the first). An auditor who quotes the hierarchy sounds like a safety professional, not a safety tourist.

🔍 Audit method — ISO 19011, and audit vs inspection

The PD blends two disciplines: inspections (field checks of workmanship, safety, compliance against standards) and audits (system checks of subcontractors and internal processes per the monthly focus). Knowing the difference — and quoting ISO 19011 — is what a trained auditor sounds like.

Inspection

AS/NZS ISO/IEC 17020 — inspection bodies
  • Examines a thing — workmanship, a site, a claim — against requirements
  • Output: pass/fail, BIF, claim verdict — evidence-based
  • The pre/post/WIP inspection regime is inspection work

Audit

ISO 19011:2018 — auditing management systems
  • Examines a system — a subcontractor's or process's management controls
  • Output: conformity / nonconformity / OFI findings against criteria
  • The monthly "auditing of subcontractors and internal staff and processes" is audit work

The ISO 19011 audit lifecycle

  1. Initiate — audit program, objectives, scope, criteria; the monthly Inspection & Audit Focus sets these.
  2. Prepare — document review, checklist, plan; contact the auditee (subcontractor or process owner).
  3. Conduct — opening meeting, evidence collection (documents, interviews, observation — and on this contract, site verification), findings.
  4. Report — findings classified: conformity, nonconformity, OFI; conclusions; distributed to the right stakeholders.
  5. Complete — records retained; in WMS against the relevant Work Order where applicable.
  6. Follow-up — corrective actions verified to closure — the post-inspection / re-audit loop.

The seven audit principles — say three, it's enough

Integrity
Professionalism, honesty — the foundation of audit trust.
Fair presentation
Report truthfully and accurately — findings, conclusions and obstacles.
Due professional care
Judgment in proportion to the task — the auditor's decisions matter.
Confidentiality
Information security — tenant data and commercial data stay protected.
Independence
Impartial and objective — especially auditing subcontractors you also work alongside.
Evidence-based approach
Findings are verifiable — "if it's not in WMS, it didn't happen" is an evidence rule.
Risk-based approach
Focus audit effort where risk is highest — the monthly audit focus in action.

📚 Cheat sheet

Six checkpoints, one master table, and the ordering traps. For the 60 seconds before the interview.

The six checkpoints

  1. Plan to KPI. Pre-inspections meet Contract KPI minimums; post-inspections on ALL pre-inspections; WIP/post to zone targets.
  2. Inspect to the standard. Workmanship (trade standard), site safety (SWMS), contractor compliance (licence + contract).
  3. Raise a BIF for every non-conformance. Specifics, evidence, owner, due date — Downer's improvement framing, not blame.
  4. Close with verify. Corrective AND preventive actions; escalate in writing before the due date; verify on site.
  5. Record in WMS. Against the Work Order, updated as actions complete — if it's not in WMS, it didn't happen.
  6. Report & trend. Monthly report with commentary + trend analysis, formally presented, feeding the next audit focus.

🚨
Safety first — an incident: make safe → notify → preserve → investigate. Never document before people are safe.
📝
Escalate early — anything threatening a due date is escalated in writing before the due date, to the SDP Manager and Regional Manager.
⚠️
The three things interviewers probe: ① the ordering — make safe before documenting, escalate before the due date, verify before closure. ② the WMS discipline — records against the Work Order, not in notebooks. ③ the tenant — closures aren't closed until the tenant is advised. Get those three right and the rest is detail.
